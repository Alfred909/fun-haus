Origins of an experience designer

Imagined places, practical questions

My interest in experience design began with places I imagined as a child. I wanted to make those places real, and would try to work out how their spaces, stories and technology could fit together. One early idea—a laser-tag environment in a shopping mall with different monsters—began as a dream.

Fun Haus grows from that same question: how can an imagined place become something other people can enter, explore and experience? Its purpose is to provide a setting where ideas can be prototyped, tested and shared.

A practice that connects disciplines

Theater and media production contribute an understanding of staging, timing and visual communication. Historical research informs the study of cultures and places. Architectural design makes those ideas spatial, while real-time environments and simulation make it possible to explore interactions before a permanent experience is built.

My work connects those disciplines through a common process: understand the idea, develop the space, define the interaction, consider how it operates, and test how the pieces work together.

Conventions, photography and observation

Convention operations and event photography bring together production and observation. A temporary entertainment environment must be assembled, used, maintained and removed; the public experience is one stage of that wider process.

The PRGE 2019 documentary in this portfolio pairs event photographs with an illustrative process model. It presents setup, public activity and teardown as connected phases, while keeping photographed evidence distinct from modeled movement and timing.

Portfolio and research in development

Fun Haus walkable portfolio

An explorable Three.js environment organizing work into a lab for interactive programs, an office for information and research, and a gallery for visual work. Visitors can select bulletin-board subjects and wall displays from a first-person viewpoint.

The lab launches the PRGE documentary after confirmation and provides an immediate exit back to the saved room position. The interface explores how a portfolio can function as a place rather than a sequence of pages.

Beyond the Building / convention geography

An interactive research presentation developed for a data-analysis class, examining convention activity beyond a single venue through maps, documented events, photographs and conceptual bomb-chart diagrams.

The work distinguishes geographic location from conceptual relationships, and treats incomplete historical coverage, illustrative models and documented observations as different forms of evidence.
